What I take off your desk

Everything below is the work that needs care and legal training, but not a licensed attorney's hour. All of it is prepared for your review and sign-off.

Legal research & memoranda

A defined legal question answered in a structured memo (issue, brief answer, facts, analysis, conclusion), with every citation checked against the source so you can verify in minutes.

  • Case law, statute and regulatory research
  • Judgment summaries, headnotes and case briefs
  • Comparative notes across US states or UK jurisdictions

Drafting & document review

First drafts and clean-ups from your precedents and instructions, formatted to house style and proofread before they reach your desk.

  • Correspondence, demand letters and engagement letters
  • Contracts, NDAs and agreements from template
  • Pleadings and motions: formatting, exhibits, tables of authorities, citation checks

Case, calendar & document management

Matters set up and kept current in your practice management system, with court dates and limitation periods flagged well before they fall due.

  • Docketing, deadline tracking and calendar management
  • File organisation, document indexing, discovery and e-filing support
  • Time entries, billing support and invoice preparation

Client intake & communication

A professional first point of contact, so new enquiries are answered the same business day and existing clients are kept informed in your voice.

  • Intake questionnaires, conflict checks and follow-up
  • Inbox management and appointment scheduling
  • Client status updates and reminders

Practice administration & executive support

The whole administrative layer that keeps a small practice running: the legal-specific work and the general executive support that usually falls to whoever is nearest.

  • Transcription and summaries of hearings, depositions and meetings
  • Inbox management, diary control and meeting coordination
  • Travel booking, itineraries and expense tracking
  • Spreadsheets, databases, data entry and reporting
  • CRM and contact list maintenance
  • Presentation decks, document formatting and template building
  • Supplier, vendor and third-party correspondence
  • Research and reporting on non-legal business questions

Questions attorneys usually ask

Straight answers to the practical things.

What is a legal virtual assistant, and how is it different from a paralegal?

A legal virtual assistant provides remote legal and administrative support under an attorney's supervision: research, drafting, case management and intake. The scope overlaps with a paralegal's, but the work is delivered remotely, on flexible hours, and always for attorney review rather than as independent legal advice.

Can you work in my time zone?

Yes. I work from West Africa Time (UTC+1), which covers the full UK working day and US Eastern mornings. Deadlines and hearing dates are tracked in your time zone, not mine.

You are based in Nigeria. How does that work for data protection?

For UK and EU firms, transfers to Nigeria need a lawful transfer mechanism. I work under a data processing agreement with the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum in place, access files only inside your systems, and keep nothing locally. I am glad to work through this with your compliance lead before anything is signed.

How do you handle confidentiality?

I sign an NDA before any file access, work only inside the systems you approve, and never store client material elsewhere. You can revoke access at any time.

How are you paid, and what does it cost?

Either a fixed weekly-hours retainer or a per-project quote, agreed before work starts, invoiced through platforms that support international payment. Rates depend on scope; the discovery call is where we settle that.

Do you use AI in your work?

Where it speeds up a first pass, yes, and never as the final word. Every citation, quotation and fact is independently verified against the primary source before anything reaches you.
